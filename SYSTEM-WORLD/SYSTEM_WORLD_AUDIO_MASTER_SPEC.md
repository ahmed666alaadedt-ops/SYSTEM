---

# 1. CORE AUDIO VISION

SYSTEM WORLD must have a realistic, immersive, adaptive and cinematic audio system.

Audio must respond to:
- Player actions.
- Missions and objectives.
- Combat and danger.
- Exploration and discoveries.
- World identity and location.
- Weather and environmental conditions.
- Time of day and seasons.
- Story events and emotional situations.

The audio system must not rely on one continuous background music track.

It must create a believable sound environment that changes naturally with the player's experience.

Realism is the primary objective.
Cinematic enhancement is used when the scene requires it.

A documented audio feature is not considered implemented until it has been programmed, integrated and tested.

---

# 2. PROGRAM OPENING — SYSTEM INTRODUCTION

The program opening and world-entry opening are two separate experiences.

The SYSTEM WORLD program opening must have its own original audio identity.

Required audio sequence:

1. A quiet cinematic beginning.
2. Deep atmospheric sound.
3. Subtle energy and electronic effects.
4. Original SYSTEM logo appearance sound.
5. Cinematic impact when the logo is revealed.
6. Appropriate transitions between introductory scenes.
7. Sound effects accompanying cinematic combat shots.
8. Original music that gradually builds in intensity.
9. Mayar appearance sound and voice, when voice content is available.
10. Smooth transition into the main application interface.

Audio must follow the exact timing of visual events.

The opening must not sound like a generic game trailer.

All music and effects must be original or properly licensed.

---

# 3. WORLD ENTRY OPENING — DREAM SEQUENCE

The dream sequence is separate from the program opening.

It introduces the player's personal journey into the virtual universe.

Required sound progression:

## 3.1 Dream beginning
- Mysterious atmospheric ambience.
- Subtle wind.
- Distant energy sounds.
- Soft cinematic music.
- Spatial environmental effects.

## 3.2 Four-world introduction
Each world must have its own audio identity.

The transitions between worlds must use appropriate sound effects and musical changes.

## 3.3 Dream ending
- Dream ambience gradually fades.
- Alarm clock becomes clearly audible.
- The alarm must feel like a real physical alarm in the player's room.

## 3.4 Waking up
- Natural morning ambience begins.
- Birds singing.
- Distant outdoor activity.
- Gentle environmental wind.
- Realistic transition from dream audio to everyday life.

The dream must not share the same continuous audio atmosphere as the waking scene.

# 12. SPATIAL 3D AUDIO

The system must support positional audio.

Required behavior:
- Sound direction.
- Distance attenuation.
- Occlusion by walls and obstacles.
- Indoor and outdoor acoustic differences.
- Cave and tunnel echoes.
- Large hall reverberation.
- Underwater filtering.
- Distant environmental sounds.
- Moving sound sources.

A sound behind the player should be perceived behind the player when the output device supports spatial positioning.

Sound should become quieter and less detailed as its source becomes distant or obstructed.

Headphone and stereo behavior must be tested separately.

---

# 13. UNDERWATER AUDIO

Underwater environments require a separate audio profile.

Required:
- Reduced high-frequency detail.
- Muffled external sounds.
- Bubbles.
- Swimming movement.
- Diving movement.
- Underwater creatures.
- Water pressure ambience.
- Surface transition effects.

The transition between above-water and underwater audio must be gradual and convincing.

---

# 14. AUDIO TRANSITIONS AND MIXING

Audio layers must work together without becoming chaotic.

Required:
- Smooth fade-in and fade-out.
- Crossfading between music tracks.
- Dynamic volume adjustment.
- Priority for important gameplay sounds.
- Dialogue clarity.
- Reduced background music during important conversations.
- Temporary music reduction during critical sound cues.
- Prevention of excessive simultaneous sound sources.

The system must avoid sudden volume spikes.

---

# 15. AUDIO SETTINGS AND ACCESSIBILITY

Provide independent controls for:

- Master volume.
- Music.
- Environmental ambience.
- Sound effects.
- Combat.
- Dialogue.
- Voice chat.
- UI sounds.

Additional options:
- Mute individual categories.
- Subtitle support.
- Visual indicators for important sound events.
- Reduced-intensity audio mode.
- Optional spatial audio settings.
- Save user preferences.

Important gameplay information must not depend exclusively on hearing.

---

# 16. PERFORMANCE AND DEVICE SUPPORT

The audio system must support mobile devices and future higher-end platforms.

Requirements:
- Efficient audio resource loading.
- Controlled number of simultaneous audio sources.
- Distance-based sound management.
- Audio streaming for large files where appropriate.
- Reuse of sound resources.
- Quality settings.
- Memory management.
- Reduced audio workload on weaker devices.

Quality must be scalable without removing essential gameplay information.

---

# 17. AUDIO ASSET POLICY

All audio assets must be:
- Original recordings.
- Original compositions.
- Properly licensed assets.
- Assets with documented usage rights.

Maintain an asset register containing:
- Asset name.
- Source.
- License.
- Usage restrictions.
- Intended world or scene.
- Technical format.

Do not use copyrighted music or sound recordings without permission.

# 20. ACCEPTANCE AND QUALITY RULES

The audio system is not complete until:

1. The program opening has its own audio sequence.
2. The dream sequence has a separate audio identity.
3. The alarm and waking transition work correctly.
4. All four worlds have distinct audio identities.
5. Music responds to missions and combat.
6. Environmental audio matches the actual environment.
7. Player movement and interactions produce suitable sounds.
8. Spatial audio behavior has been tested.
9. Audio settings save correctly.
10. Accessibility alternatives work.
11. Mobile performance is acceptable.
12. All audio assets have verified usage rights.
13. No major audio errors or unwanted overlaps remain.

Never mark a feature complete based only on written documentation.

# 21. FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

SYSTEM WORLD must not merely play sounds.

It must create the feeling that the player is physically present in a living world.

A peaceful forest should feel peaceful.
A dangerous cave should create tension.
A battle should feel powerful.
A discovery should feel meaningful.
A city should feel inhabited.
The sea should feel alive.
The dream should feel mysterious.
The waking world should feel real.

Realism first.
Emotional immersion second.
Cinematic enhancement when appropriate.

This document is the central audio design reference for SYSTEM WORLD.

All future audio implementation must follow this specification unless a deliberate design revision is approved.
