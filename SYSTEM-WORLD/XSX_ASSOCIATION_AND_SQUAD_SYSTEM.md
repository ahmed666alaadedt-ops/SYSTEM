SYSTEM WORLD — XSX ASSOCIATION AND SQUAD SYSTEM

Document Type: Core Game System Specification
Status: Approved Design Foundation
Project: SYSTEM WORLD
Organization: XSX Hunter Association

---

1. Core Identity

XSX is the one official Hunter organization across the four independent worlds of SYSTEM WORLD.

XSX is simultaneously:

- The official Hunter association.
- The central organization for Hunter registration and licenses.
- The organization responsible for Hunter examinations.
- The central mission and contract authority.
- The home of all Hunter squads and teams.
- The organization responsible for Hunter ranks, records, and achievements.

There must not be a separate competing Hunter Guild system outside XSX.

The XSX identity and emblem are part of the main SYSTEM visual identity.

Membership is optional. Players may explore, live, work, trade, and play independently without joining XSX.

2. XSX Headquarters and Branches

XSX operates through:

- A central headquarters.
- Regional branches.
- World-specific offices.
- Examination facilities.
- Mission and contract boards.
- Training facilities.
- Medical and recovery services.
- Hunter records and registration facilities.
- Squad facilities and meeting areas.

The organization operates across all four worlds:

1. Modern World.
2. Historical World.
3. Fantasy World.
4. Greed Island.

Each world may have different branches, facilities, missions, and local rules, while remaining part of the same XSX organization.

3. Hunter License and Hunter Card

Every registered Hunter receives a unique Hunter identity.

The Hunter Card is the player's central identity and progression document.

It contains:

- Unique Hunter ID.
- Player name.
- XSX emblem.
- License status.
- Hunter rank.
- Examination history.
- Achievements.
- Personal abilities.
- Mission records.
- Rewards and completed contracts.
- Relevant organization permissions.

The card must be accessible through the player's interface and XSX facilities.

A player receives an official Hunter License only after successfully completing the required examination.

4. Hunter Examination

The Hunter Examination is an original multi-stage qualification experience.

It may include:

- Long-distance running and endurance.
- Forest and mountain survival.
- Navigation and exploration.
- Dangerous terrain.
- Monster encounters.
- Individual and team challenges.
- Sea travel and ship survival.
- Storms and marine creatures.
- Resource gathering.
- Observation and intelligence tests.
- Puzzles and problem solving.
- Combat and tactical challenges.
- Final qualification stages.

Examination stages may change between examination periods.

Experienced Hunters with suitable qualifications may become examiners, supervisors, or trainers.

The examination is optional and must not prevent players from enjoying the rest of the world.

Passing the examination grants the official XSX Hunter License and access to the relevant Hunter privileges.

5. Hunter Progression and Personal Abilities

Hunter progression is personal.

Each Hunter has independent:

- Level.
- Experience.
- Rank.
- Abilities.
- Equipment.
- Achievements.
- Mission history.

After qualification, players may unlock access to a unique personal energy and ability development system.

This system may take broad inspiration from the concept of personal energy abilities, but all classifications, names, mechanics, progression, and balancing must be original to SYSTEM WORLD.

Not every Hunter receives identical abilities.

Abilities depend on:

- Training.
- Player decisions.
- Achievements.
- Special examinations.
- Personal development.
- Relevant world rules.

The ability systems of the four worlds remain distinct.

6. XSX Mission and Contract System

XSX manages missions through its official mission boards and the Hunter Card.

Mission categories include:

- Monster hunting.
- Monster tracking.
- Boss elimination.
- Exploration and area discovery.
- Dangerous-zone investigation.
- Caravan and location protection.
- Rescue operations.
- Resource collection.
- Dungeon and Gate missions.
- Raids.
- Investigation and information gathering.
- Special challenges.
- World-specific contracts.

Missions may be:

- Solo missions.
- Squad missions.
- Multi-squad operations.
- Organization-wide events.

Players may accept missions individually or invite other Hunters.

Each mission must clearly display:

- Mission title and description.
- Required objectives.
- Location.
- Difficulty and danger level.
- Recommended Hunter level or rank.
- Required participants, if applicable.
- Rewards.
- Mission status.
- Contribution records.

Mission states may include:

- Available.
- Accepted.
- Active.
- Completed.
- Failed.
- Suspended, when supported by the mission rules.

Rewards may include XP, currency, equipment, crafting materials, ability manuals, rare items, and achievements.

Rewards must reflect mission difficulty, successful completion, and individual contribution.

Repeated exploitation and reward duplication must be prevented.

7. XSX Squad System

A Squad is an operational team inside XSX.

A Squad is not a separate Guild or independent Hunter organization.

Each Squad has its own identity and records.

Every Squad contains:

- Unique Squad ID.
- Custom Squad name.
- Custom Squad emblem.
- Squad leader.
- Deputy leader.
- Members.
- Squad colors and visual identity.
- Squad specialization.
- Squad level.
- Squad experience.
- Squad reputation.
- Victory and achievement records.
- Mission history.

The Squad leader manages the team and its operations.

The deputy leader supports the leader and may receive delegated permissions.

Squad membership and leadership permissions must be clearly defined.

8. Squad Emblem Evolution

Every Squad has its own emblem.

The emblem is not a permanently fixed image.

Its appearance evolves as the Squad progresses, inspired broadly by the visual progression of clan badges in strategy games.

Possible visual progression stages:

1. Basic.
2. Bronze.
3. Silver.
4. Gold.
5. Diamond.
6. Legendary.

Visual evolution may include:

- New borders.
- Additional decorative details.
- More advanced symbols.
- Special materials.
- Lighting effects.
- Animated effects at high stages.
- Unique legendary visual elements.

The emblem must preserve the Squad's identity and remain recognizable as it evolves.

Emblem evolution is cosmetic and symbolic. It must not automatically grant combat power.

9. Independent Squad Progression

Squad level is independent from:

- XSX organization level.
- Individual Hunter levels.

Example:

- Squad level: 40.
- Squad leader: Hunter level 35.
- Member: Hunter level 20.
- New member: Hunter level 5.

These levels must remain independent.

A Squad reaching level 40 does not automatically raise the levels or combat power of its members.

Squad XP is earned through legitimate collective achievements:

- Completing XSX missions.
- Hunting monsters and bosses.
- Exploring dangerous regions.
- Completing successful raids.
- Achieving team objectives.
- Completing advanced contracts.

The system must prevent abuse through repeated member switching, artificial mission completion, or reward farming.

10. Squad Unlocks and Benefits

Squad progression may unlock:

- More difficult mission categories.
- Advanced hunting contracts.
- Rare monster contracts.
- Higher-risk operations.
- Special raids.
- Squad decorations.
- Banners and flags.
- Advanced Squad facilities.
- Squad headquarters upgrades.
- Special titles.
- Competitive rankings.
- Additional organizational services.

Higher-level activities may offer greater rewards because of their increased difficulty and requirements.

Squad level alone must not grant unfair combat advantages.

Rewards must remain connected to challenge difficulty and actual participation.

11. XSX, Squad, and Hunter Progression Layers

SYSTEM WORLD uses three independent progression layers:

Layer A — XSX Organization

- Organization level.
- Organization reputation.
- Organization experience.
- Headquarters development.
- Branches and facilities.
- Organization-wide achievements.

Layer B — Squad

- Squad level.
- Squad experience.
- Squad reputation.
- Squad emblem evolution.
- Squad achievements.
- Squad missions and facilities.

Layer C — Individual Hunter

- Personal level.
- Personal XP.
- Hunter rank.
- Personal abilities.
- Equipment.
- Personal achievements.
- Hunter Card and license.

No progression layer automatically replaces or controls another.

12. Friends and Party System

The Friends system is separate from XSX membership.

Players can:

- Search by player name.
- Search using a unique player ID.
- Send friend requests.
- Accept or reject requests.
- Remove friends.
- Block players.
- Invite friends to a Party.
- Invite friends to activities and missions.

A Party is a temporary or persistent player group for cooperative play, not a Squad and not an organization.

Players may invite friends before entering a world or while already playing.

Friends do not need to be in the same world when the invitation is sent.

After accepting an invitation, the game may offer a supported transition to the leader's world or activity.

Party size depends on the activity:

- Solo.
- Two players.
- Three players.
- Four players.
- Five players.
- Six players.

Normal exploration and cooperative play must not have an unnecessary forced timer.

The team may continue exploring, trading, visiting cities, or accepting another mission after completing an activity.

Some special challenges and seasonal events may have their own time limits.

13. Shared Team Exploration

When supported by the activity, Party members can:

- Explore open-world environments together.
- Fight monsters together.
- Complete missions together.
- Discover locations.
- Visit cities and facilities.
- Travel between supported destinations.
- Participate in raids.

The shared map should display:

- Party member locations.
- Member movement.
- Directions.
- Distance.
- Markers.
- Meeting points.

If a member disconnects or leaves, the remaining players may continue when the mission rules allow it.

Returning members may rejoin at an appropriate location and mission state when supported.

Individual rewards and progress must reflect each player's participation and contribution.

14. Multi-Squad Operations

Some high-level XSX missions may require multiple Squads.

These operations may include:

- Large monster hunts.
- World boss encounters.
- Dangerous Gate operations.
- Large-scale raids.
- Rescue operations.
- Special organization events.

Each Squad retains its own identity, leader, records, and progression.

Individual Hunters also retain their own contribution records and rewards.

15. Organization Reputation and Fair Progression

XSX reputation develops through legitimate organizational achievements.

Squad reputation develops through team activity and successful operations.

Hunter reputation develops through personal actions and achievements.

The system must prevent:

- Artificial XP farming.
- Duplicate rewards.
- Unfair member rotation.
- Fake mission completion.
- Exploitation of squad progression.
- Paid advantages that undermine fair competition.

Optional memberships or purchases may support cosmetics, convenience, or storage, but must not sell exclusive combat superiority.

16. Interface Requirements

The XSX interface should provide access to:

- Hunter Card.
- Hunter profile.
- License and rank.
- Mission board.
- Examination information.
- Friends.
- Party.
- Squad profile.
- Squad members.
- Squad emblem.
- Squad progression.
- Squad mission history.
- Organization reputation.
- Headquarters and branch information.

All interfaces must be designed for the project's native landscape orientation.

The interface must remain readable and usable on mobile devices.

17. Technical Architecture and Implementation Rules

The XSX system should be developed as modular components.

Suggested future components:

- "xsx_association_manager.gd"
- "xsx_hunter_profile.gd"
- "xsx_hunter_license_manager.gd"
- "xsx_mission_manager.gd"
- "xsx_squad_manager.gd"
- "xsx_squad_emblem_progression.gd"
- "xsx_friend_party_manager.gd"

These are proposed components, not a declaration that these files already exist.

Before creating each component:

1. Inspect the current repository.
2. Check for existing equivalent functionality.
3. Reuse existing managers where appropriate.
4. Avoid duplicate systems.
5. Preserve the current world managers and NPC systems.
6. Integrate through clear interfaces.
7. Keep multiplayer and cloud synchronization in mind.
8. Preserve individual and Squad progression independently.

Player identity, Squad membership, mission completion, and progression will eventually require reliable server-side validation and persistent cloud storage.

Client-side values must not be trusted as the sole authority for valuable rewards or competitive progression.

19. Non-Negotiable Design Rules

1. XSX is the single Hunter organization across the four worlds.
2. No separate Hunter Guild exists outside XSX.
3. Squads belong to XSX and are not independent Guilds.
4. Every Squad has its own name, emblem, leader, deputy, members, and records.
5. Squad emblems evolve with Squad progression.
6. Squad level is independent from Hunter levels.
7. XSX organization progression is independent from Squad progression.
8. Hunter progression remains personal.
9. Friends and Party are separate from Squad membership.
10. Players retain the freedom to play alone.
11. Cooperative play supports open-world exploration, not only small match arenas.
12. Mission rewards reflect difficulty and contribution.
13. Progression must resist exploitation.
14. All four worlds retain their independent identities and rules.
15. All interfaces are designed in landscape orientation.
16. Existing project systems must be preserved during implementation.

End of XSX Association and Squad System Specification.
