
# SYSTEM WORLD
# MASTER PROJECT SPECIFICATION
# Version 2.0

---

## 1. PROJECT VISION

SYSTEM WORLD is an immersive virtual universe combining realistic life simulation, fantasy worlds, exploration, combat, social interaction, professions, progression, and cinematic storytelling.

The project must provide a connected experience where users can choose their own path, explore independent worlds, build their characters, develop abilities, interact with other inhabitants, and experience different forms of virtual life.

All systems described in this document are part of one unified project.

Inspiration from existing games and fictional universes must remain inspiration only. SYSTEM WORLD must maintain its own identity, original content, characters, systems, and implementation.

---

## 2. WORLD STRUCTURE

SYSTEM WORLD consists of:

1. The main realistic virtual world.
2. Four independent fantasy worlds.
3. The Star Gates and World Transition System.
4. The Monster Gates and Dungeon System.
5. The Instant Travel System.
6. The transportation and travel systems.
7. Shared character progression, inventory, missions, and rewards.

Each world maintains its own identity, geography, environment, rules, inhabitants, and activities.

---

## 3. MAIN WORLD — HUNTER-INSPIRED EXPERIENCE

The main fantasy experience is inspired by the general world structure of Hunter x Hunter.

It includes:

- Hunter examinations.
- Multi-stage tests and challenges.
- Hunter registration and official licenses.
- Hunter organizations and associations.
- Government relationships and regulations.
- Hunter ranks and progression.
- Missions, contracts, and rewards.
- Exploration and dangerous territories.
- Rare discoveries and valuable items.
- Character development and special abilities.

The Hunter examination must be a substantial experience with multiple stages, challenges, and different types of tests.

Successful candidates receive recognition and access to advanced opportunities.

The Hunter Association operates within an organized relationship with government institutions.

This world is inspired by the general concept, not a direct reproduction of copyrighted characters, locations, or stories.

## 7. GANGS AND UNDERWORLD

The world contains organized gangs and underground activities.

Players may encounter:

- Organized criminal groups.
- Rival gangs.
- Underground markets.
- Secret missions.
- Dangerous territories.
- Conflicts between organizations.

## 8. MONSTER GATES AND DUNGEON SYSTEM

Monster Gates are an independent system inspired by the general dungeon and monster-gate concept of Solo Leveling.

These gates appear throughout the world and can be discovered and entered.

Inside them, players can find:

- Monsters.
- Powerful bosses.
- Dangerous environments.
- Hidden areas.
- Exploration opportunities.
- Combat challenges.
- Resources and materials.
- Treasure and rare equipment.
- Missions and special rewards.

Players can enter these gates, fight monsters, explore their interiors, collect resources, and complete objectives.

Each gate has its own:

- Difficulty level.
- Danger classification.
- Monster population.
- Boss encounters.
- Environmental characteristics.
- Entry requirements.
- Rewards.

Players develop their characters through these experiences by improving their levels, strength, skills, weapons, and equipment.

Monster Gates are separate from Star Gates. They are not the same system and must never replace one another.

---

## 9. STAR GATES AND WORLD TRANSITION SYSTEM

Star Gates are a fundamental independent system in SYSTEM WORLD.

Their primary purpose is connecting different worlds, maps, locations, islands, and historical eras.

Star Gates are distributed across cities, regions, special locations, and hidden areas.

Some gates are visible, while others require discovery.

### 9.1 Gate classifications

Star Gates may have:

- Different ranks.
- Different power levels.
- Special requirements.
- Entry restrictions.
- Rare keys.
- Special missions.
- Discovery conditions.
- Legendary classifications.

Some gates may require a specific player level, completed mission, achievement, key, or special ability.

### 9.2 Types of transitions

The system supports:

1. Spatial transition:
   Moving between locations and regions.

2. World transition:
   Traveling between independent worlds and maps.

3. Time transition:
   Traveling to different historical eras and returning to the past or other periods.

4. Mythological transition:
   Entering mythological, legendary, and fantasy worlds.

### 9.3 Gate discovery

Players may discover gates through:

- Exploration.
- Visiting specific locations.
- Missions.
- Achievements.
- Special clues.
- Hidden keys.
- Puzzles and secret conditions.

Some gates remain undiscovered until the player fulfills their requirements.

### 9.4 Instant travel

Instant travel is a related but separate capability.

It allows rapid movement within the same world or between permitted locations.

Its use depends on:

- Player abilities.
- Progression.
- Energy or resource requirements.
- Location restrictions.
- Special unlock conditions.

Some advanced instant-travel abilities are rare and require substantial progression.

### 9.5 Cinematic gate transitions

Crossing a Star Gate should create a cinematic experience.

It may include:

- Visual energy effects.
- Environmental transformations.
- Spatial distortion.
- Gate activation animations.
- Atmospheric sound effects.
- Destination previews.
- Arrival sequences appropriate to the destination.

### 9.6 Independent world identity

Each connected world keeps its own:

- Map.
- Geography.
- Rules.
- Environment.
- Creatures.
- Characters.
- Activities.
- Progression opportunities.

Star Gates connect these worlds without removing their independence.

### 9.7 Natural transportation remains available

Star Gates do not replace ordinary travel.

Cars, trains, ships, and aircraft remain important transportation methods in the realistic virtual world.

### 9.8 Important system separation

The following systems must remain distinct:

- Star Gates: connecting worlds, maps, islands, and eras.
- Monster Gates: monster areas, dungeons, combat, exploration, and rewards.
- Instant Travel: a rapid movement capability with conditions.
- Transportation: natural physical travel using vehicles.

### World Four — GREED ISLAND

The fourth world is known as Greed Island.

Its initial presentation uses:

- A dark background.
- A faint island silhouette.
- A mysterious atmosphere.
- The letter S positioned in the middle as a hidden clue.

The presentation must suggest that the island contains an undiscovered secret.

The mystery should be revealed progressively through exploration and gameplay.

The other fantasy worlds retain their previously approved identities and designs.

## 15. OPENING STORY — DREAM SEQUENCE

The initial entry into SYSTEM WORLD uses a cinematic story introduction.

The player begins with a dream.

During the dream:

- The player experiences a mysterious environment.
- The four fantasy worlds appear.
- Each world is introduced through cinematic visual scenes.
- Mysterious energy and environmental effects accompany the scenes.
- The dream creates curiosity about the worlds and their secrets.

The dream ends with the sound of the player's alarm clock.

The player wakes up.

After waking:

- Natural environmental sounds become audible.
- Birds can be heard.
- The atmosphere changes from dreamlike to realistic.
- The player transitions toward the world-entry experience.

This opening should create the feeling of beginning a personal adventure.

It may be inspired by the general emotional impact of adventure-game introductions such as Where Winds Meet, without copying its scenes, story, or characters.

- Hunters can discover and enter Monster Gates.
- Star Gates connect independent worlds.
- Police can investigate criminal activity.
- Civilian players can work and participate in daily life.
- Players can travel using ordinary transportation.

## 24. TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT PRINCIPLES

Development must follow these principles:

- Modular architecture.
- Independent systems with clear responsibilities.
- Reusable components.
- Persistent player data.
- Reliable world-transition handling.
- Clear separation between gameplay logic and interface.
- Scalable world content.
- Error handling.
- Performance optimization.
- Mobile compatibility.
- Testing before integration.

Existing project files must be inspected before modification.

New functionality must integrate with existing code rather than unnecessarily replacing working systems.

A documented feature is not considered implemented until its code, integration, and tests are completed.

## 26. FINAL PROJECT RULE

This document is the central reference for SYSTEM WORLD.

- Star Gates.
- Monster Gates.
- Instant travel.
